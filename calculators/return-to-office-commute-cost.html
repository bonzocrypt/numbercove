How this calculator works

Daily costs

Driving can be figured two ways: fuel (miles ÷ mpg × gas price) plus per-mile maintenance and depreciation, or the IRS business mileage rate of 76¢ a mile for July through December 2026, which also covers insurance and other fixed costs. Tolls, parking, transit, childcare, meals, and other office-day costs are added per day.

Annual cost = (daily costs − daily savings) × office days a year + monthly costs × 12

The raise you'd need

Commuting costs come out of take-home pay and generally aren't deductible for employees, so a raise must cover the tax on the raise too.

Raise = annual cost ÷ (1 − combined tax rate)

Your time

Round-trip minutes × office days gives unpaid hours. They're valued at your salary's hourly rate (salary ÷ 2,080) unless you enter your own figure.

Sources

  1. IRS: 2026 business standard mileage rate (72.5¢ from January 1)
  2. CPA Practice Advisor: IRS raises the business rate to 76¢ for July 1 to December 31, 2026
  3. U.S. Energy Information Administration: weekly retail gasoline prices ($4.28 national average, week of October 19, 2026)

Last reviewed 2026-10-27. Rules, rates, and prices change; check the source for your exact situation.

Common questions

How much does returning to the office cost?

It depends on distance and extras, but driving 30 miles round trip three days a week with parking and lunch commonly runs several thousand dollars a year before counting your time.

How much of a raise do I need to return to the office?

Divide your annual commuting cost by one minus your combined tax rate. At a 30% tax rate, $5,000 of commuting costs needs about a $7,140 raise.

Can I deduct my commute?

Generally no. Commuting between home and your regular workplace is a personal expense for employees under federal tax rules.
