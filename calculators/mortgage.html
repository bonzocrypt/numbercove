Mortgage payment calculator

Enter the home price, down payment, rate, and term to get monthly principal and interest, total interest, a payoff month, and the first year of amortization. Optional tax, insurance, HOA, and PMI estimate a full housing payment.

How this calculator works

Monthly principal and interest uses the standard fixed-rate formula. M = P · r(1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n − 1) where P is price minus down payment, r is the monthly rate, and n is the number of months. Extra principal is applied after the scheduled payment and shortens the loan. PMI is estimated only while the modeled down payment is under 20%.

The full housing estimate can include principal, interest, property tax, homeowners insurance, HOA dues, and estimated PMI. Only principal reduces the loan balance; escrowed taxes and insurance remain housing costs but do not build equity. The amortization snapshot shows why fixed-rate payments are interest-heavy early and principal-heavy later.

This is not a lender quote. It does not include every closing cost, mortgage insurance rule, escrow adjustment, special assessment, tax reassessment, rate lock, or loan feature. Adjustable-rate, interest-only, balloon, and biweekly products require different modeling. Compare the result with a formal loan estimate and leave room for maintenance, utilities, and other ownership costs.

A worked example: the default $400,000 home

With 20% down, the loan is $320,000. At 6.5% over 30 years, principal and interest come to $2,022.62 a month. Adding the default property tax ($4,800 a year, or $400 a month) and insurance ($1,400 a year, or $116.67 a month) brings the housing payment to $2,539.28. No PMI applies because the down payment is 20%.

Over 30 years that loan costs $408,142 in interest, more than the amount borrowed. Most of it is paid early: the 1st payment sends $1,733.33 to interest and just $289.28 to principal.

Each half point moves the payment by roughly $100 a month on this loan, or $37,000 to $38,000 over 30 years. That is why it pays to get quotes from several lenders on the same day and compare the Loan Estimates line by line.

15 years vs. 30 years, and extra payments

  • A 15-year term at the same 6.5% raises the payment to $2,787.54 but cuts total interest to $181,758, saving about $226,000. In practice 15-year loans usually carry lower rates, which widens the gap.
  • An extra $200 a month on the 30-year loan pays it off in about 23 years and 5 months and saves $105,429 in interest, while keeping the lower required payment as a fallback if money gets tight.

PMI: what it costs and when it ends

With less than 20% down on a conventional loan, lenders usually require private mortgage insurance. This calculator assumes 0.5% of the loan per year if you leave the field blank; actual rates depend on credit score and down payment. On a $360,000 loan with 10% down, 0.5% is $150 a month.

Under the federal Homeowners Protection Act, you can ask to cancel PMI on a conventional loan once the balance reaches 80% of the home's original value, and it must end automatically at 78% if you are current on payments. FHA loans work differently: their mortgage insurance premium generally lasts for the life of the loan when the down payment is under 10%.

Checking affordability

A long-standing lender guideline keeps the total housing payment at or below about 28% of gross monthly income. The $2,539.28 payment in the example works out to an income of about $108,800 a year. Lenders also look at total debt payments, and approval amounts are often higher than what is comfortable. Leave room for maintenance, often budgeted at 1% or more of the home's value a year, plus utilities and repairs the payment does not cover.

Mortgage questions

What is the monthly payment on a $320,000 mortgage?

At 6.5% over 30 years, principal and interest are $2,022.62 a month. Taxes, insurance, HOA dues, and PMI are added on top.

When does PMI go away?

On a conventional loan, you can request cancellation when the balance reaches 80% of the home's original value, and it ends automatically at 78% if payments are current. FHA mortgage insurance follows different rules.

Is a 15-year mortgage better than a 30-year?

A 15-year loan costs far less interest but has a much higher payment. On $320,000 at 6.5%, it saves about $226,000 in interest but raises the payment by about $765 a month.

This is a planning estimate, not a lender quote. Actual payments vary with points, prepaid interest, escrow cushions, mortgage insurance rules, and rounding. Not financial advice.
