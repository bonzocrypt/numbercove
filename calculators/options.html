How the option prices and probabilities are calculated

European options (most index options such as SPX, NDX, and RUT) are priced with the Black-Scholes-Merton formula with a continuous dividend yield. American options (stocks and ETFs) can be exercised early, so they are priced with a Leisen-Reimer binomial tree, using Richardson extrapolation across 2 tree sizes. A call with no dividend is never worth exercising early, so it uses Black-Scholes, which is exact in that case. American Greeks are measured by small bumps to price, time, and volatility on the tree.

Time runs in calendar days, with each option expiring at the 4:00 p.m. Eastern close. A target date of today values the position at the current moment; later dates value it at that day's close.

Implied volatility drives the price. If you leave IV blank, it is solved from the leg's market price (mid, or the side you entered, or your custom entry price). The IV slider moves leg 1 to the value shown and shifts every other leg by the same number of points. The IV crush button lowers leg 1's IV by the percentage you enter and shifts the other legs by the same points. Broker Greeks you enter are for comparison only; the calculator re-derives Greeks from the model and flags large differences.

Breakeven, maximum profit and loss, and probability of profit are measured at the first expiration in the position. On that date, expiring legs are worth their intrinsic value and any later legs keep their time value at the scenario IV. Probability of profit uses a lognormal price distribution at the expiring legs' entered IV, with drift equal to the risk-free rate minus the dividend yield. That is a model-implied (risk-neutral) estimate, not a forecast. For calendar and diagonal spreads, legs that expired before the target date are valued at their intrinsic value at the scenario price.

Entry cost uses the basis you choose: mid, a conservative fill (buy at the ask, sell at the bid), the bid, or the ask. A custom entry price overrides it. With no market price, the model's value today is used and labeled.

Educational estimate only. Not investment advice or a recommendation to buy or sell any security. Model values assume constant volatility and rates between now and the target date and ignore discrete dividend dates, early assignment risk, commissions, and bid/ask spreads at exit. Real option prices can differ materially. Options involve risk and are not suitable for all investors.
