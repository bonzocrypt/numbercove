Repair or replace calculator for appliances

The 50% rule is a start, but it ignores age, energy use, and the chance the repair holds. This compares the true yearly cost of fixing versus replacing, and charts the most a repair is worth as the appliance ages.

How this calculator works

Yearly cost of replacing

A new unit's price, plus install and haul-away less rebates and scrap value, is spread over its lifespan with an interest rate (the capital recovery factor), then its yearly energy cost is added.

Replace per year = net price × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−life) + new kWh × price

Yearly cost of repairing

A repair buys the remaining life of the appliance if it holds. If it doesn't, we assume the unit fails again within about six months. The expected life bought is:

Life after repair = chance it lasts × years left + (1 − chance) × 0.5

The repair is spread over that life the same way, plus the old unit's energy use and any extra upkeep.

Most a repair is worth

The break-even repair is the amount that makes both yearly costs equal. Any quote below it favors repairing. The chart repeats this at every age, which is why the line falls: an older unit has fewer years left to spread a repair over.

Typical lifespans

Presets use midpoints of InterNACHI's estimated life expectancy ranges and typical installed prices. Your model, usage, water quality, and maintenance can move them a lot, so edit them freely.

Common questions

What is the 50% rule for appliance repair?

If a repair costs more than half the price of a new appliance, replace it. It's a quick screen, but it ignores how many years the appliance has left and how much a new one would save on energy. The calculator shows the 50% rule next to the fuller answer.

How long do appliances last?

Roughly: dishwashers about 9 years, washers 10, refrigerators 10 to 13, dryers about 13, ranges 13 to 15, tank water heaters 8 to 10, furnaces 15 to 25, and central air conditioners 10 to 12. Use the preset as a starting point and adjust for your model.

Does a new appliance's energy savings matter?

For refrigerators, freezers, HVAC, and water heaters, it can. Compare the kWh on the yellow EnergyGuide label for each unit. For small appliances, energy savings rarely change the answer.

Should I repair an appliance that's under 5 years old?

Usually yes. With most of its life ahead, a repair is spread over many years, so even a sizable quote can be worth paying. Check whether the manufacturer's or a credit card's extended warranty still covers it.
