Loan and amortization calculator

Works for auto, personal, and other fixed-rate installment loans. Add extra principal to see how many months and how much interest you cut.

How this calculator works

Scheduled payment uses monthly compounding: interest each month is balance times rate/12. Extra principal is applied after that interest and reduces the balance faster. The table lists the first twelve payments plus the final payment after extra principal.

Each payment is divided between interest and principal. Early in a typical fixed loan, the larger balance creates more interest; later payments send more to principal. Entering an extra monthly amount lets you compare the scheduled payoff with an accelerated payoff and see how much modeled interest the extra principal avoids.

The estimate assumes a fixed rate, equal monthly periods, on-time payments, and no payment fees. It does not model an adjustable rate, deferred interest, daily simple interest, late charges, prepayment penalties, taxes, insurance, or origination costs. Confirm how your lender applies extra payments and whether it advances the due date instead of reducing principal.

How the term changes the cost

Using the calculator's default loan of $25,000 at 7.25%, here is what each common term costs. A longer term lowers the payment, but every extra year adds about $1,000 of interest.

TermMonthly paymentTotal interest
36 months (3 years)$774.79$2,892
48 months (4 years)$601.56$3,875
60 months (5 years)$497.98$4,879
72 months (6 years)$429.23$5,905
84 months (7 years)$380.38$6,952

The 84-month loan pays $117.60 less per month than the 60-month loan but costs $2,073 more in interest. On a car, the longer term carries a 2nd risk: cars lose value fastest in their first years, so a long loan can leave you owing more than the car is worth for much of the term. That matters if the car is totaled or you want to trade it in early.

Where each payment goes

Interest is charged on the balance you still owe, so early payments are interest-heavy. On the default loan, the 1st payment of $497.98 includes $151.04 of interest and only $346.94 of principal. As the balance falls, the interest portion shrinks and more of each payment pays down the loan.

What extra payments are worth

Adding $100 a month to the default loan pays it off in 49 months instead of 60 and saves $976 in interest. Every extra dollar goes straight to principal, which stops interest from being charged on it for the rest of the loan.

2 things to confirm with your lender before paying extra:

  • It is applied to principal. Some lenders treat an overpayment as an early payment of next month's bill unless you say otherwise. Look for a "principal only" option.
  • There is no prepayment penalty. Most auto and personal loans have none, but check the contract.

Rate vs. APR, and how interest is charged

The interest rate is what you are charged on the balance. The APR adds certain fees, such as an origination fee, spread over the loan, so it is the better number for comparing offers. Enter the interest rate here and compare the APR across lenders separately.

This calculator assumes interest is charged monthly. Many auto loans use daily simple interest instead, where interest builds each day between payments. The totals are very close if you pay on time; paying a few days early saves a little, and paying late costs a little more than shown.

Loan questions

What is the monthly payment on a $25,000 loan?

At 7.25% over 60 months, about $497.98. Over 36 months it is $774.79, and over 72 months it is $429.23.

Is it better to take a shorter loan term?

A shorter term costs less interest overall: on $25,000 at 7.25%, 36 months costs $2,892 in interest versus $4,879 over 60 months. The trade-off is a higher monthly payment.

How much do extra payments save?

On a $25,000 loan at 7.25% over 5 years, an extra $100 a month pays it off 11 months early and saves about $976 in interest.

Estimate only. Fees, compounding conventions, and prepayment penalties are not modeled. Not financial advice.
