Pay off the mortgage early or invest?

Extra payments earn a guaranteed return equal to your mortgage rate. Investing might earn more. See exactly where the line is with your numbers.

DB
By Dan Belliveau · Former senior equities and options trader turned product builder

The answer

Enter your numbers to see the answer.

How this calculator works

Two strategies

  • Pay off early: add the extra amount to every payment until the loan is gone, then invest the full payment plus the extra for the rest of the original term.
  • Invest the extra: keep the regular schedule and invest the extra every month.

Both end at the original payoff date with no mortgage, so the comparison is the investment balance.

Returns and taxes

The investment return is an annual rate converted to monthly. Tax drag lowers it for taxable accounts. If you itemize, the interest deduction's tax savings are invested in both strategies.

Break-even return

The calculator searches for the return at which both strategies end with the same net worth. Because a mortgage rate compounds monthly, the break-even annual return is slightly above the stated rate (a 6% mortgage equals about 6.17% a year).

Sources

  1. IRS Publication 936: Home mortgage interest deduction

Last reviewed 2026-10-27. Rules, rates, and prices change; check the source for your exact situation.

Common questions

Is it better to pay off my mortgage early or invest?

If your investments earn more than your mortgage rate after taxes, investing builds more wealth on average. Paying off the mortgage is a guaranteed return with no market risk. The calculator shows the break-even return for your loan.

What is the break-even return?

The annual investment return at which investing the extra and paying off the loan early leave you with the same net worth at the original payoff date.

Does the mortgage interest deduction change the answer?

Only if you itemize deductions. It lowers your effective mortgage rate, which makes investing more attractive.
